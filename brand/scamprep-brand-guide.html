Brand guide. Lamplight replaces Signal Amber. October 2026.

Amber is the light left on at home.

The same paper, type, and color as before, now steady and warm instead of sweeping and watchful. No radar, no dark surfaces, no surveillance tone.

1

The idea

Why it fits

Adult children get a brand that feels like a living room in the evening, not a control room. Amber now reads as light, so it reassures instead of alerting. Older adults get large, plain Manrope at calmer weights, and no figure drawings that could caricature them: illustrations show only household objects. Advisors see restraint: one typeface, one accent, product screens doing most of the talking. It is also the cheapest move. Paper, amber, deep amber, the radius scale, and the spacing scale all survive, so the live site can change in a day.

Do

  • Use amber as light: soft fills, glows, and the one primary button.
  • Keep walnut ink for text and thin lines only.
  • Draw objects from a home: a lamp, a table, a mug, a phone.

Do not

  • Do not animate amber in loops. A light that blinks is an alarm.
  • Do not use walnut ink as a background, even in the footer.
  • Do not draw people or faces.

Where it can drift

The drift here is toward generic and a little cold. Without the radar, one sans and one accent can look like any tidy startup, and amber still means caution to most people. The guard is to keep amber large, soft, and still, and to give coaching its own dusk blue so amber never says "careful." Illustration risk is low for cutesy and higher for clip art. Hold to three objects per scene, one 2.5px line, and amber as the only strong fill. No faces, sparkles, or steam.

9

Illustration

These are examples of the style, not a fixed set or a placement plan. Draw new ones the same way wherever the brand needs warmth: a page section, an email header, the printed report, a welcome card.

  • Line based. One 2.5px walnut line with round joins. Amber is the only strong fill, with glow and honey behind it as light.
  • Subjects come from a home: a lamp, a table, a chair, a door, a letter, a phone. Three objects per scene at most.
  • People are never depicted. That removes any chance of caricature and keeps the focus on calm surroundings.
  • Use it with a light hand. Product screens still do most of the showing.
10

Motion

  • The glow settles once. On load, the primary button's halo fades up over 900ms and then holds still. Nothing pulses or loops.
  • In the report, lights come on one at a time, 80ms apart, once, when scrolled into view.
  • Hover and focus changes take 150ms, ease-out.
  • With reduced motion on, everything appears in its final state. No fades, no stagger.
